How to Pay Off Debt Faster With a Cashback Lump Sum

One of the fastest ways to cut the cost of high-interest debt is a lump-sum payment: a single extra payment that goes straight to the balance. A lump sum reduces the balance that interest is charged on from that day onward, so it keeps saving you money every month after you make it.

This guide shows, with real numbers in Canadian dollars, how much a lump sum of $250 to $1,500 can save on a typical credit card balance. It also explains how Credit Pulse members can use their activation cashback, budgeting tools and debt tools to do exactly that.

Key takeaway: On a $5,000 credit card balance at 20.99% with $200 monthly payments, a one-time $1,000 payment saves about $666 in interest and gets you debt-free 9 months sooner, as long as you keep making the same $200 payments afterward. Try your own numbers in the free Debt Payoff Calculator.

Why a lump sum works so well on credit card debt

Credit card interest is charged on your balance, so a lump sum saves you money in two ways:

  1. Every dollar of the lump sum stops collecting interest immediately. At about 21%, each $100 removed from a card balance avoids roughly $1.75 of interest in the first month alone, and that keeps going for every month the balance would have existed.
  2. Your regular payment works harder. With a smaller balance, less of each monthly payment goes to interest and more goes to the balance itself, so the debt shrinks faster from then on.

The earlier you make the lump-sum payment, and the higher the interest rate on the debt it goes toward, the more it saves.

Your cashback-to-debt plan in 6 steps

  1. List your debts with balances, interest rates and minimum payments.
  2. Pick the target: usually the debt with the highest interest rate, which for most people is a credit card. That is the debt avalanche method, explained in our guide to debt repayment strategies in Canada.
  3. Apply the full lump sum to the target as soon as you receive it, before it gets absorbed into everyday spending.
  4. Keep your regular payment the same. The savings in the table depend on it; if you lower your monthly payment after the lump sum, most of the benefit disappears.
  5. Don't run the card back up. The freed-up credit is not new money.
  6. Re-run your plan in the Debt Payoff Calculator to see your new debt-free date.

Other sources of lump sums

The same strategy works with any one-time money: a tax refund, a work bonus, money from selling things you no longer use, or a gift. Decide in advance that one-time money goes to your highest-interest debt, and the savings add up quickly.

Frequently asked questions

Is it better to make a lump-sum payment or increase monthly payments?

Both help, and doing both is best. A lump sum saves the most when it is made early and applied to your highest-interest debt, as long as you keep your regular payment the same afterward.

How much interest does a $1,000 lump sum save on a credit card?

In our example, a $5,000 balance at 20.99% paid at $200 a month, a one-time $1,000 payment saves about $666 in interest and cuts 9 months off repayment.

Which debt should I put a lump sum toward?

Usually the debt with the highest interest rate, which for most people is a credit card. This is the debt avalanche approach.

Can I use my Credit Pulse cashback to pay off debt?

Yes. Cashback is paid when your membership is activated, and you can use it however you choose, including as a lump-sum payment on high-interest debt. Make sure the membership's biweekly payments also fit your budget.

Will paying off a credit card lump sum improve my credit?

Lowering your balance reduces how much of your available credit you are using, which is one factor in credit scores. Results vary, and Credit Pulse does not provide credit repair.

What other one-time money can I use as a lump sum?

Tax refunds, work bonuses, money from selling things you no longer use, and gifts all work. Decide in advance that one-time money goes to your highest-interest debt.
